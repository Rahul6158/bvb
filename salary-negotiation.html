2. Exact Scripts & Word-for-Word Responses Recruiter Dialogues

When the Recruiter Asks: "What are your salary expectations?"

First Response (Don't volunteer a premature number):

"I'm primarily looking for the right role and a good technical fit. Since this position is closely aligned with my experience, I'd be looking for compensation that's competitive with the role and the level you're considering me for. I'm happy to discuss the range once we have more clarity on the role."

If They Insist on a Number:

"Based on the role and my experience in production data extraction and backend systems, I'd be targeting something around the low-to-mid twenties in total compensation, but I'm flexible depending on the overall structure and level."

If You Want to Give a Concrete Range:

"I'd be looking at around ₹22–26 LPA total compensation, depending on the fixed and variable components."
Why this works: Gives you room to negotiate without making an unrealistic or rigid demand.

When Asked About Your Current Salary

Never Say: "I'm currently earning X, so I expect a 50% hike." Your existing salary should never be your primary benchmark when bridging to a Tier-1 firm like D.E. Shaw.

Recommended Professional Framing:

"My current compensation is based on my existing role and company. For this move, I'm more interested in the responsibilities and market level of the position. Since this role is directly aligned with my production experience in data extraction, Python and distributed systems, I'd prefer to discuss compensation based on the role and the value I can bring."

(If they require the exact number for verification, provide it honestly without inflating it.)

3. Counter-Offer Scenarios & The "Silence Technique" Response Playbook

Scenario A: If They Offer ₹18 LPA

Don't immediately accept or reject. Use the pause & push technique:

"Thank you. I'm definitely interested in the opportunity. I was hoping we could explore whether there's some flexibility in the compensation, considering my hands-on experience with production-scale data extraction, AWS, SQS and distributed pipelines."
Crucial Tactic: Stop talking immediately after asking. Let the recruiter break the silence and respond first.

Scenario B: If They Offer ₹20 LPA

Nudge gently toward your target band:

"I'm very interested in the role. Based on the responsibilities and the experience I bring in data extraction and backend systems, I was hoping we could get a little closer to the ₹23–25 LPA range. Is there flexibility in the package?"
Notice the subtle psychology: Don't demand "I need ₹25L". Ask "Is there flexibility?" — it keeps the conversation open and collaborative.

Scenario C: If They Offer ₹23–25 LPA

This is already a strong, meaningful offer. Rather than pushing blindly for a higher top-line number, shift your focus to deconstructing the breakdown:

"Could you help me understand the fixed component, variable component and any other benefits included in the CTC?"
4. Deconstructing CTC: ₹25 LPA CTC ≠ ₹25 LPA Fixed Compensation Math

What to Inspect Before Signing

A quoted CTC can look very different in monthly bank credit. Always clarify these 8 elements:

  • Fixed / Base Pay: The guaranteed monthly salary component (aim for ≥ 70-80% of total).
  • Performance Bonus: Is it guaranteed in year one, or strictly linked to company/individual performance?
  • Joining / Sign-on Bonus: Is it paid in Month 1? Is there a clawback clause (e.g. 1-year retention)?
  • Annual Bonus: What is the historical payout percentage for this band?
  • Retention / Deferred Components: Is part of the CTC paid only after 2 or 3 years?
  • Timing of Payouts: When is the variable component disbursed (annually, semi-annually)?
  • Relocation / Benefits: Medical insurance coverage, food/travel allowances, retirement benefits (PF/Gratuity).
  • Target vs Guaranteed: Is the figure an achievable target or an optimistic upper bound?
5. Your Strongest Arguments vs Fatal Mistakes Negotiation Leverage

• Don't say: "I really need a higher salary because of personal expenses."
• Don't say: "My current company pays me very little, so I need a big jump."

Your Strongest Negotiation Argument

"I already have production experience in the exact problem area you're hiring for."

Remind them of what you have built in production, not just studied academically:

  • Reverse engineering internal REST/GraphQL APIs and bypassing bot protection.
  • Overcoming TLS and JA3 fingerprinting using curl_cffi.
  • Solving distributed race conditions with SQS visibility timeouts and Redis locking.
  • Decoupled AWS architecture (Lambda, SQS, EC2, PostgreSQL, MongoDB, Redis, Grafana).

  • "32 Gunicorn Workers": Explain the CPU-to-worker core formula and why EC2 persistent workers replaced Lambda for session-heavy extraction.
